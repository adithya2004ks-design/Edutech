Q1: A disaster is a sudden and severe event that causes significant damage to life, property, and the environment. Disasters can be classified as natural, such as floods and earthquakes, or man-made, such as industrial accidents. The causes of disasters include natural processes like tectonic movements and extreme weather conditions. Human activities such as deforestation, unplanned urbanization, and pollution also increase the risk. These factors together make communities more vulnerable to disasters.

Q2: Ozone depletion is mainly caused by the release of harmful chemicals into the atmosphere. Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs), commonly used in refrigerators and aerosol sprays, are the primary contributors. These chemicals break down ozone molecules in the stratosphere. Other substances like halons and nitrogen oxides also damage the ozone layer. As a result, the protective layer becomes thinner and allows more ultraviolet radiation to reach the Earth.

Q3: A hazard is a situation or condition that has the potential to cause harm to people, property, or the environment. Hazards are generally classified into natural and man-made types. Natural hazards include events like earthquakes, floods, and cyclones. Man-made hazards include industrial accidents, fires, and pollution. There are also biological hazards such as diseases and epidemics that affect human health.

Q4: Contemporary approaches to risk assessment involve systematic methods to identify and reduce disaster risks. These approaches use scientific data and modern tools such as Geographic Information Systems (GIS). They also include community participation and awareness programs. Risk assessment considers factors like vulnerability, exposure, and capacity of people. The main goal is to minimize damage and improve preparedness for disasters.

Q5: Public health services are essential to protect people during and after disasters. Important services include emergency medical care, safe drinking water, and proper sanitation facilities. Disease surveillance and control measures are also necessary to prevent outbreaks. Vaccination programs help protect vulnerable populations from infections. In addition, mental health support and counseling services are important for recovery.
